The basics

How an oral film works

An oral film looks like a small clear sticker. You place it in your mouth and it does the rest.

  1. Step 1

    Place it in the mouth

    On the tongue, the gum or the inside of the cheek. No water, nothing to swallow.

  2. Step 2

    It dissolves

    Saliva softens the film. The design of the film decides how fast it lets go of each ingredient.

  3. Step 3

    Medicine enters the blood

    Ingredients pass through the lining of the mouth, the oral mucosa, straight into the bloodstream.

Works fast

Absorption through the mouth lining starts quickly.

Precise dosing

Controlled delivery gives better stability and can lower the dose needed.

Skips the liver's first pass

Avoiding first-pass metabolism means fewer side effects across the body.

Hard to misuse

A fixed dose per film helps prevent overdose.

Easy for every patient

Suits children, older adults and people who find swallowing hard (dysphagia).

Discreet and portable

Fits in a wallet. Use it anywhere, without water.

Why films stayed small

Four limits kept oral films in a niche. P/ODF removes them.

Films have been on pharmacy shelves for years, yet most medicines could never use them. A standard film holds too little, dissolves in minutes, handles one ingredient and is costly to make.
